2Assessing the Operational Feasibility of Evolutionary Therapy in Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer
Journal of Evolutionary Biology · IF 2.3 · 2026-10-30
📄 Evolutionary cancer therapy (ECT) applies principles of evolutionary game theory to prolong the effectiveness of cancer treatment by curbing the development of treatment resistance. It was shown to increase time to progression while decreasing the cumulative drug dose. ECT individually tailors treatment schedules for patients based on their cancer dynamics and, thus, requires regular follow-up and precise measurements of the cancer burden. The current literature on ECT often overlooks clinical realities, such as rather long intervals between tests, possible appointment delays and measurement errors, in the development of the treatment protocols. In this study, we assess the feasibility of ECT for metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) under clinically realistic follow-up constraints. We create virtual patients with cancer dynamics described by the polymorphic Gompertzian model, based on data from the START-TKI clinical trial. We assess the effects of longer test intervals, measurement error and appointment delays on the expected time to progression under the evolutionary therapy protocols. We show that a higher containment level, although it increases time to progression in the model's predictions, may lead to premature treatment failure in the presence of measurement error and appointment delay. Further, the ECT protocol with a single containment bound is demonstrated to be more robust to imprecise tumor burden measurements and delayed appointments than the protocol with two bounds. Finally, we show that a dynamically adjusted treatment protocol can be beneficial for individual patients, but requires a thorough follow-up. This study contributes to the design of a clinical trial and the future clinical implementation of evolutionary therapy for NSCLC.

International Journal of Molecular Sciences · IF 4.9 · 2026-10-30
📄 Anabolic-androgenic steroids (AAS) comprise testosterone and synthetic androgenic compounds that differ substantially in pharmacokinetics, receptor activity, route of administration, hepatic metabolism, and clinical risk. From a forensic perspective, the central problem is not simply to catalogue adverse effects but to determine how verified exposure, chronology, co-use of other substances, clinical and electrocardiographic findings, pathological alterations, and toxicological results should be integrated when evaluating an individual death. This critical narrative review therefore distinguishes therapeutic testosterone replacement from non-medical and supraphysiological AAS exposure and examines the evidence connecting exposure to endocrine, cardiovascular, hepatic, renal, musculoskeletal, neuropsychiatric, and immunological findings. The detailed immunological literature is predominantly mechanistic, in vitro, or experimental and currently provides limited direct forensic evidence in human AAS users. Similarly, Raman spectroscopy is best regarded as a developing adjunct for forensic trace or analytical applications rather than a validated method for confirming AAS accumulation or cause of death in post-mortem tissue. We propose a structured forensic framework in which exposure is first verified, the temporal and pharmacological context is reconstructed, findings are assessed for specificity and plausibility, co-exposures and competing causes are examined, and the final opinion explicitly communicates the degree of certainty and the limitations of the evidence.

1Occupational Radiation Exposure During Robotic-Assisted Navigational Bronchoscopy: A Prospective Real-Time Dosimetry Study.
Journal of bronchology & interventional pulmonology · IF 3.2 · 2027-01-01 · PubMed
📄 Robotic-assisted bronchoscopy (RAB) uses fluoroscopy and cone-beam CT (CBCT), which can expose procedural staff to ionizing radiation. Real-time, role-specific occupational dosimetry data from live RAB procedures remain limited. We prospectively collected continuous personal dosimetry from attending physicians, anesthesia providers, and scrub nurses during 51 consecutive RAB procedures at a single academic center using a standardized low-dose imaging protocol and shielding. Collar-badge readings were converted to estimated effective dose using a conservative upper-bound conversion factor. Role-specific dose distributions were compared, and scenario-based annual estimates were generated by bootstrap resampling. Median per-procedure estimated dose was 1.27 µSv for attending physicians, 0.58 µSv for anesthesia providers, and 0.55 µSv for scrub nurses. Exposure differed by role, with the primary operator receiving the highest dose. CBCT cases were associated with higher staff and patient radiation measures but also involved smaller lesions and longer fluoroscopy times, indicating confounding by procedural complexity. Patient BMI was independently associated with physician exposure. Projected annual exposures remained well below the ICRP 20 mSv occupational limit across all roles. Occupational radiation exposure during RAB was low across all staff roles under an optimized imaging workflow. Role-specific dosimetry captures exposure differences across the procedural team and supports standardized low-dose imaging and shielding as core safety practices.
