1Sexual dimorphism shapes the serum metabolome profile of Parkinson’s disease patients harboring rare genetic variants of uncertain pathogenicity
npj Parkinson's Disease · IF — · 2026-11-24
📄 Recent findings indicate that sex and genetic background affect serum metabolic profiles in patients with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease (iPD) and in those with genetic PD (gPD) carrying pathogenic variants. However, blood metabolic features in patients harbouring rare variants of uncertain pathogenicity remain poorly understood. We combined 1 H-Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) metabolomics and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to characterise serum profiles in 336 clinically and genetically defined PD patients (iPD, gPD, and rare-variant PD [rvPD]) and 137 sex-matched healthy controls (HCs). In conclusion, our findings indicate that sexual dimorphism shapes systemic metabolic alterations in rvPD and that their circulating amino acid profiles combine features shared across idiopathic and genetic PD subtypes.

📄 Structural variants (SVs) contribute substantially to genomic variation and disease, but detecting somatic SVs (sSVs) remains difficult due to reference bias, mosaicism, and enrichment in repetitive regions. 3Contextual Bandits and Optimistically Universal Learning
Mathematics of Operations Research · IF 1.9 · 2026-11-24
📄 We consider the contextual bandit problem in general action and context spaces. This generalizes the standard multiarmed bandit to the case where side information is available—for example, patients’ records or customers’ history—which allows for personalized treatment. We focus on consistency (vanishing regret compared with optimal policy) and show that for large classes of non-independent and identically distributed (non-i.i.d.) contexts, consistency can be achieved regardless of the reward mechanism, a property known as universal consistency. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work giving consistent algorithms for any contextual bandits instance with finite action spaces and i.i.d. contexts. Further, we first give necessary and sufficient conditions on the context-generating process for universal consistency to be possible. Second, we show that there always exists an algorithm that guarantees universal consistency whenever this is achievable, referred to as optimistically universal. Interestingly, for finite action spaces, the processes admitting universal learning are exactly the same as in the full-feedback supervised setting: learning can be performed with partial feedback without any generalization cost. The algorithms balance a trade-off between generalization (similar to structural risk minimization) and personalization (tailoring actions to specific contexts). Lastly, we investigate the benefit of continuity assumptions on rewards for universal learning. Physical Review A · IF 2.9 · 2026-11-24
📄 Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a targeted cancer treatment that uses light-activated photosensitizers to generate reactive oxygen species that selectively destroy tumor cells, generally causing less collateral damage than conventional treatments. However, its clinical success hinges on the availability of photosensitizers with strong optical sensitivity and high efficiency in generating reactive oxygen species. While classical computational methods have provided useful insights into photosensitizer design, they struggle to scale and often lack the accuracy needed for these simulations. In this work, we show how fault-tolerant quantum algorithms can help identify promising photosensitizer candidates for PDT. First, we introduce a threshold projection algorithm that estimates the dipole strength within the therapeutic window as a measure of optical sensitivity. Two readouts per execution reduce the absorption sampling cost at the same combined filtering and statistical accuracy. These algorithms directly evaluate photophysical quantities that can guide photosensitizer design.

Gynecological endocrinology : the official journal of the International Society of Gynecological Endocrinology · IF 1.7 · 2026-12-31 · PubMed
📄 To evaluate contraceptive effectiveness, discontinuation rates and reasons, and bleeding patterns among women who continued using the levonorgestrel 52-mg intrauterine device (LNG 52-mg IUD) for up to 10 years. We conducted a retrospective cohort study at the Universidade Estadual de Campinas School of Medical Sciences, Campinas, Brazil. We reviewed 9,236 medical records and identified women who used an LNG 52-mg IUD inserted between 2007 and 2015 for contraception and continued use for up to 10 years without replacement. Reasons for discontinuation included accidental pregnancy, bleeding and/or pain, expulsion, medical indications, expiration of the approved duration, personal reasons, and menopause. The most common reason for discontinuation was participant-requested removal at the end of the approved duration. At 10 years, approximately 30% of users reported amenorrhea and 30% regular bleeding. Continuous LNG 52-mg IUD use for up to 10 years was associated with high contraceptive effectiveness, high continuation, and a favorable bleeding profile. Prospective studies are needed to confirm the efficacy and safety of extended use beyond the currently approved duration.
