1Decoding monogenic obesity: Genetics, diagnosis, and emerging therapies
Journal of Pediatric Endocrinology and Diabetes · IF — · 2026-10-24
📄 Monogenic obesity is an uncommon but clinically actionable subset of childhood obesity, typically characterized by early-onset severe obesity and pathological hyperphagia. This narrative review summarizes clinical recognition, diagnostic evaluation, cytogenetic and molecular genetic testing, and variant interpretation in view of the evolving genetic spectrum, as well as emerging opportunities for precision therapy in monogenic non-syndromic obesity. The leptin melanocortin pathway, involving the leptin gene (LEP) , leptin receptor ( LEPR ), pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) , proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type-1 (PCSK1) , and melanocortin-4 receptor ( MC4R ) genes, remains central to the pathogenesis of monogenic obesity, with additional contributions from genes including SH2B adaptor protein (SH2B1) 1, single-minded homolog 1, Kinase suppressor of Ras 2 (KSR2), and GNAS complex locus (GNAS) , as well as emerging genes such as prolyl 4-hydroxylase, transmembrane (P4HTM) and Bassoon presynaptic cytomatrix protein (BSN) . Genetic evaluation should be guided by obesity onset before the age of 5 years, marked hyperphagia, family history of consanguinity, and associated syndromic or endocrine features rather than by the severity of obesity alone. Recent exome-wide studies highlight the limitations of restricted gene panels and support broader genomic approaches when clinical suspicion remains high. Molecular diagnosis has important therapeutic implications—metreleptin provides hormone replacement in congenital LEP deficiency, whereas setmelanotide activates the downstream MC4R pathway in selected genetic forms of obesity. Rigorous variant interpretation and functional correlation remain essential, as the presence of a rare variant alone does not establish causality or therapeutic eligibility. Recognition of monogenic obesity is, therefore, increasingly important because a molecular diagnosis can guide prognosis, genetic counseling, family testing, and mechanism-based treatment. As the genetic and therapeutic landscape expands, monogenic obesity is evolving from a primarily diagnostic entity into an increasingly actionable group of disorders, amenable to therapy.

1Mean-Field Limit of Non-exchangeable Multi-agent Systems Over Hypergraphs with Unbounded Rank
Journal of Nonlinear Science · IF 2.6 · 2026-10-23
📄 Interacting particle systems are known for their ability to generate large-scale self-organized structures from simple local interaction rules between each agent and its neighbors. In addition to studying their emergent behavior, a main focus of the mathematical community has been concentrated on deriving their large-population limit. In particular, the mean-field limit consists of describing the limit system by its population density in the product space of positions and labels. The strategy to derive such limits is often based on a careful combination of methods ranging from analysis of PDEs and stochastic analysis, to kinetic equations and graph theory. In this article, we focus on a generalization of multi-agent systems that includes higher-order interactions, which has largely captured the attention of the applied community in the last years. In such models, interactions between individuals are no longer assumed to be binary (i.e., between a pair of particles). Instead, individuals are allowed to interact by groups so that a full group jointly generates a nonlinear force on any given individual. The underlying graph of connections is then replaced by a hypergraph, which we assume to be dense, but possibly nonuniform and of unbounded rank. For the first time in the literature, we show that when the interaction kernels are regular enough, then the mean-field limit is determined by a limiting Vlasov-type equation, where the hypergraph limit is encoded by a so-called UR-hypergraphon (unbounded-rank hypergraphon), and where the resulting mean-field force admits infinitely-many orders of interactions.

arXiv · IF — · 2026-10-22
📄 Recent methods for injecting graph structure into LLM prompts include graph soft prompting, in which a graph neural network (GNN) learns graph-specific tokens that capture relational information among entities in textual prompts. G-Retriever combines graph soft prompts with a verbalized description of the same graph to strengthen the textual prompt signal. We conduct an in-depth analysis of G-Retriever and uncover a pathological behavior we term token collapse. Across three benchmarks, three graph encoders, two LLM families, and both frozen and LoRA-adapted models, G-Retriever's graph tokens become almost indistinguishable across inputs. We provide evidence that the same failure is inherited by subsequent methods, even with multi-token pooling. Under the standard setup, swapping a token with the dataset mean, a token from another example, or one derived from a corrupted graph produces no resolved answer-level effect in accuracy. Moreover, a learned constant prompt that never accesses the graph matches or surpasses G-Retriever on all three benchmarks. These findings indicate that, rather than conveying graph content as intended, the collapsed token functions primarily as a generic soft prompt. We also examine conditions linked to this collapse and propose a two-stage procedure that yields non-collapsed tokens while maintaining competitive performance in most cases. However, token variability alone does not prove that the model uses graph information: via interventions and decoding probes, we show that depending on the configuration, a token can reflect the question, an answer prior, or graph-derived content that the LLM may or may not use. Overall, our results offer a practical approach to evaluating graph-token alignment rather than inferring it from task accuracy alone. Code to reproduce our experiments is available at https://github.com/difra100/Token_Collapse_KGQA.

8Geometry-Dependent Properties of Physically Crosslinked PPy-PVA Hydrogels Enable Tunable Iontophoretic Drug Delivery
arXiv · IF — · 2026-10-22
📄 Objective: Iontophoretic drug delivery has primarily relied on current modulation to regulate drug transport. Here, we investigate hydrogel geometry, microstructure, and local electric fields as alternative mechanisms for controlling transdermal delivery. Methods: Bovine serum albumin (BSA) was encapsulated in polypyrrole-polyvinyl alcohol (PPy-PVA) hydrogels. Five geometries, including Hilbert curve-based designs, were fabricated to vary pore architecture and local electric field distributions. We optimized PPy content, drug loading, and current density. Electric fields were evaluated using finite element analysis, and microstructures were characterized by scanning electron microscopy. Drug transport was assessed using Franz diffusion cells with artificial membranes. Results: Hydrogel geometries exhibited distinct microstructures and electric field distributions. Iontophoresis substantially enhanced permeability compared with passive diffusion, while individual geometries produced different cumulative release, flux, and permeability profiles. The average iontophoretic permeability was (3.80 +/- 1.93) x 10^-5, exceeding previously reported values using artificial membranes. Conclusion: Geometry-driven fabrication of drug-loaded hydrogels enables tunable transport characteristics under both iontophoretic and passive conditions. Significance: Geometry-mediated control offers an alternative approach to regulating iontophoretic drug delivery without requiring individual current-density modulation.
