📄 The silicon microelectrode ‘Utah’ array has enabled people with paralysis to control computer cursors, operate prosthetic limbs and communicate using high-performance brain–computer interfaces. 4Active Jurin’s law
Journal of Fluid Mechanics · IF 3.9 · 2026-11-25
📄 Capillary rise is a classical interfacial-flow problem in which capillary suction at a curved meniscus is balanced by viscous resistance, inertia and gravity. Here, we formulate a continuum theory for the capillary rise of active nematic fluids. Starting from the active-fluid momentum equation and the interfacial normal stress balance, we derive a one-dimensional active Bosanquet equation for the rise height. The active stress contributes an additional normal traction at the liquid–gas interface and thereby renormalises the effective capillary driving pressure. In the static limit, this gives a generalised active Jurin’s law controlled by an active Jurin number, which compares the active normal stress with the classical Laplace pressure. Depending on the sign and magnitude of this number, and on the interfacial alignment of the active constituents, activity can enhance, reduce or suppress capillary rise, and can even drive imbibition in nominally non-wetting capillaries. The early-time dynamics shows that a constant active normal stress preserves the classical Lucas–Washburn (LW) exponent, while modifying the prefactor through the active Jurin number. State-dependent active feedback changes the steady-height structure: linear weak-response feedback produces only a single dissipative steady height, whereas nonlinear feedback can generate multiple admissible equilibria. Linear stability analysis (LSA) shows that true activity-induced bistability requires two stable heights separated by an unstable one. Inertia does not create additional steady heights or alter their stability ordering, but controls the transient approach through overdamped, critically damped or underdamped relaxation. The theory identifies capillary rise as a simple macroscopic probe of active normal stresses, interfacial alignment and nonlinear orientational feedback in confined active fluids.

Applied to beacons from seven access points, including a body-worn receiver at six indoor sites at 5 and 6 GHz, the chain gives delay spreads of 24-96 ns; walking lowers the median correlation over one beacon interval from 0.85 to 0.15, near the estimator's floor. Unprecoded data frames gave 0.2-1.2 times the beacons' delay spread on the same channel, so the two should not be pooled.
