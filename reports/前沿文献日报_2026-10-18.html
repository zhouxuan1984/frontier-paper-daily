1Language modeling on tabular data: a survey of foundations, techniques and evolution
Artificial Intelligence Review · IF 13.9 · 2026-10-17
📄 Tabular data, a prevalent data type across various domains, presents unique challenges due to its heterogeneous nature and complex structural relationships. Achieving high predictive performance and robustness in tabular data analysis holds significant promise for numerous applications. Influenced by recent advancements in natural language processing, particularly Transformer architectures, new methods for tabular data modeling have emerged. Early techniques concentrated on pre-training Transformers from scratch, often encountering scalability issues. Subsequently, methods leveraging pre-trained language models like BERT have been developed, which require less data and yield enhanced performance. The recent advent of large language models, such as GPT and LLaMA, has further revolutionized the field, facilitating more advanced and diverse applications with minimal fine-tuning. Despite the growing interest, a comprehensive survey of language modeling techniques for tabular data remains absent. This paper fills this gap by providing a systematic review of the development of language modeling for tabular data, encompassing: (1) a categorization of different tabular data structures and data types; (2) a review of key datasets used in model training and tasks used for evaluation; (3) a summary of modeling techniques including widely-adopted data processing methods, popular architectures, and training objectives; (4) the evolution from adapting traditional Pre-training/Pre-trained language models to the utilization of large language models; (5) an identification of persistent challenges and potential future research directions in language modeling for tabular data analysis. GitHub page associated with this survey is available at: https://github.com/lanxiang1017/Language-Modeling-on-Tabular-Data-Survey.git .

2Annealed leap-point sampler for multimodal target distributions
Statistics and Computing · IF 1.6 · 2026-10-17
📄 Abstract In Bayesian statistics, exploring high-dimensional multimodal posterior distributions poses major challenges for existing MCMC approaches. This paper introduces the annealed leap-point sampler (ALPS), which augments the target distribution state space with modified annealed (cooled) distributions, in contrast to traditional tempering approaches. The coldest state is chosen such that its annealed density is well-approximated locally by a Laplace approximation. This allows for automated setup of a scalable mode-leaping independence sampler. ALPS requires an exploration component to search for the mode locations, which can either be run adaptively in parallel to improve these mode-jumping proposals, or else as a pre-computation step. A theoretical analysis shows that for a d-dimensional problem the coolest temperature level required only needs to be linear in dimension, $$\mathcal {O}\left( d\right) $$ O d , implying that the number of iterations needed for ALPS to converge is $$\mathcal {O}\left( d\right) $$ O d (typically leading to overall complexity $$\mathcal {O}\left( d^3\right) $$ O d 3 when computational cost per iteration is taken into account). ALPS is illustrated on several complex, multimodal distributions that arise from real-world applications. This includes a seemingly-unrelated regression (SUR) model of longitudinal data from U.S. manufacturing firms, as well as a spectral density model that is used in analytical chemistry for identification of molecular biomarkers.

4Impact of matched samples on domain harmonization in cross-cohort NMR metabolomics
Metabolomics · IF 3.3 · 2026-10-17
📄 INTRODUCTION: Machine learning models trained on metabolomics data frequently show reduced performance when applied to independent cohorts because of domain shifts introduced by differences in analytical platforms, sample handling, storage conditions, and metabolite quantification procedures. Robust approaches for harmonizing metabolomics datasets across cohorts are therefore needed to improve model transferability and reproducibility. OBJECTIVES: To evaluate the impact of matched samples on domain harmonization and cross-cohort prediction performance in NMR metabolomics data, and to compare multiple harmonization strategies for improving model generalization across independently acquired datasets. METHODS: A machine-learning framework was developed to classify ICD-10 code I50.9 (unspecified heart failure) using plasma NMR metabolomics data from the Estonian Biobank. Models were trained on a source dataset comprising 12,000 samples measured at Nightingale Health and evaluated on an independently measured destination dataset of 295 samples acquired on a different NMR platform. Harmonization approaches included within-domain normalization, Optimal Transport, Bridge mapping, and Empirical Bayes correction. The effect of matched samples was assessed using up to 108 duplicated biospecimens measured in both datasets. Additional validation was performed using independently generated human gestational diabetes and mouse diabetes metabolomics datasets from Metabolomics Workbench. RESULTS: Domain harmonization generally improved cross-cohort prediction compared with unaligned data, particularly when matched samples were available. Predictive performance increased as the number of matched samples used for calibration increased, although gains varied across methods. Nevertheless, simple within-domain normalization consistently provided strong performance and often matched or exceeded more complex alignment approaches. External validation using fully unmatched human and mouse metabolomics datasets demonstrated that harmonization strategies improved classification performance relative to raw data, supporting the broader applicability of the framework. CONCLUSION: Matched samples substantially enhance domain harmonization and improve the transferability of metabolomics prediction models across analytical platforms and cohorts. However, robust normalization remains a strong baseline, and the benefits of more sophisticated alignment methods depend on the availability and representativeness of matched reference samples. These findings provide practical guidance for developing reproducible cross-cohort metabolomics machine-learning models while preserving biologically relevant signals.

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising distributed learning paradigm that enables collaborative model training across multiple institutions without transferring raw patient data. Finally, current research gaps, emerging trends, and future directions for developing secure, scalable, and trustworthy federated healthcare systems are identified.

📄 Drug discovery has evolved from a largely empirical discipline into a knowledge-intensive enterprise that depends on the integration of pharmacology, structural biology, computational chemistry, and bioinformatics. Modern pharmacotherapeutic design is no longer satisfied by the demonstration that a molecule binds a macromolecular target. Instead, a candidate must be considered within a broader context that includes disease mechanism, network position of the target, tissue expression, structural tractability, off-target liabilities, pharmacokinetic behavior, and the probability of translation from preclinical systems to humans. Particular emphasis is placed on the limitations of current approaches, including database incompleteness, bias in training sets, uncertain biological annotation, and the common error of overinterpreting docking scores or predictive probabilities as proof of therapeutic success. The central argument is that the most reliable drug design strategies emerge when computational breadth is combined with pharmacological depth. In that integrative framework, bioinformatics helps define what should be tested, while pharmacology determines what the resulting signals actually mean for drug action, safety, and translation.

1Sex Differences in Wheelchair Marathon Performance.
American journal of physical medicine & rehabilitation · IF 2.4 · 2026-11-01 · PubMed
📄 The aim of the study was to evaluate the historical trends of sex differences in athletic performance and participation among male and female wheelchair marathon athletes. This retrospective, observational study utilized secondary, publicly available data of the Boston, Chicago, and New York City marathons within the wheelchair division from 1984 to 2023. Athletic performance and participation were evaluated across all marathon events and over time using univariate analysis of variance and Pearson correlations. Sex differences in athletic performance were observed with males outperforming females in all marathon events over time ( P < 0.001). The sex difference among first place finishers was 20% and increased to 33% for tenth place finishers. Although sex differences decreased over time ( P < 0.001), large sex differences in performance remain. Female athlete participation in wheelchair marathon events significantly increased over time ( P < 0.001) and the ratio of male athletes to female athletes in these events significantly decreased over time ( P < 0.001). Overall participation and male athlete participation also generally increased over time. Significant sex differences were observed in wheelchair marathon performance and participation. These findings highlight the importance of sex as a key biological variable related to human health and performance, especially among wheelchair athletes.

BMC Medicine · IF 8.3 · 2026-10-16
📄 BACKGROUND: Multiple long-term conditions (MLTC) are increasingly common and present challenges for hospital healthcare professionals (HCPs) making decisions about admissions, referrals, and care management in the context of a single-organ oriented health system. This qualitative study examined how HCPs in emergency and acute hospital settings make such decisions for people living with MLTC, exploring factors that influence clinical judgement and how HCPs respond to these challenges. METHODS: HCPs involved in making decisions about care for people living with MLTC presenting to hospital were recruited via National Health Service (NHS) research delivery networks and professional contacts, using purposive and snowball sampling. Semi-structured individual interviews were conducted, focusing on how HCPs make decisions regarding the care of people living with MLTC and factors influencing such decisions. Reflexive thematic analysis was undertaken. Themes relating to influences on decision-making were organised using the Social Ecological Model (SEM). RESULTS: We interviewed 40 HCPs working in NHS emergency departments and acute assessment units across multiple regions, specialties and roles. Seven themes were constructed and organised into two overarching categories. The first category 'Factors Influencing Decision-Making' included the themes the complex patient, professional expertise and identity, system pressures and inflexible structures. These themes were mapped to the SEM, demonstrating influences across individual and environmental levels. The second category 'Adaptive Decision-Making Strategies' included the themes Mitigating risk and Relying on heuristics capturing how HCPs navigate the uncertainty created by these influences. The theme, Relational care, operated between categories, illustrating how relationships both shape what decisions are possible (interpersonal level of SEM) and provide a means of navigating uncertainty. CONCLUSIONS: Clinical decision-making for people living with MLTC in emergency and acute settings is shaped by uncertainty arising from the interaction of factors operating across individual, interpersonal and environmental levels. To manage this uncertainty, staff employ a range of adaptive strategies. However, participants described this level of compensatory work as challenging and potentially insufficient on its own. Supporting decision-making and reducing reliance on adaptive responses may benefit from system‑level change, including improved community care, enhanced generalist expertise, opportunities for relational decision-making, better continuity and shared ownership of care, and flexible care pathways.

Using a critical literature review methodology, this research evaluates the theoretical rigor, methodological validity, and contextual relevance of the original study. The review acknowledges the novelty of applying the Phillips-Sul club algorithm to a 159-country panel, confirming that energy self-sufficiency does not converge globally but forms distinct convergence clubs predominantly driven by fossil fuel endowments.
