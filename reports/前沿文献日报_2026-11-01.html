📄 Background Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) is a debilitating psychiatric condition influenced by both genetic and environmental risk factors. Epigenetic modifications, such as DNA methylation, may offer insights into biologically meaningful differences associated with the disorder. Methods We conducted the largest methylome-wide association study of OCD to date, analyzing saliva DNA samples from 414 adults with OCD (297 female, 117 male) and 384 adult control participants (275 female, 109 male) using the Illumina EPICv2 array. Differentially methylated positions (DMPs) and differentially methylated regions (DMRs) were identified, with additional analyses including sex-stratified comparisons, methylation quantitative trait loci (mQTL) mapping, and assessments of differences in cell type composition. Correlation analyses between brain and saliva methylation indicated that several findings may reflect brain-relevant biology. Conclusions Our findings emphasize the importance of integrating epigenetic, genetic, and sex-specific data to advance our understanding of OCD. DNA methylation may ultimately contribute to progress toward clinically relevant precision medicine approaches.

2Assessing the Operational Feasibility of Evolutionary Therapy in Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer
Journal of Evolutionary Biology · IF 2.3 · 2026-10-30
📄 Evolutionary cancer therapy (ECT) applies principles of evolutionary game theory to prolong the effectiveness of cancer treatment by curbing the development of treatment resistance. It was shown to increase time to progression while decreasing the cumulative drug dose. ECT individually tailors treatment schedules for patients based on their cancer dynamics and, thus, requires regular follow-up and precise measurements of the cancer burden. The current literature on ECT often overlooks clinical realities, such as rather long intervals between tests, possible appointment delays and measurement errors, in the development of the treatment protocols. In this study, we assess the feasibility of ECT for metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) under clinically realistic follow-up constraints. We create virtual patients with cancer dynamics described by the polymorphic Gompertzian model, based on data from the START-TKI clinical trial. We assess the effects of longer test intervals, measurement error and appointment delays on the expected time to progression under the evolutionary therapy protocols. We show that a higher containment level, although it increases time to progression in the model's predictions, may lead to premature treatment failure in the presence of measurement error and appointment delay. Further, the ECT protocol with a single containment bound is demonstrated to be more robust to imprecise tumor burden measurements and delayed appointments than the protocol with two bounds. Finally, we show that a dynamically adjusted treatment protocol can be beneficial for individual patients, but requires a thorough follow-up. This study contributes to the design of a clinical trial and the future clinical implementation of evolutionary therapy for NSCLC.

7Abuse of Anabolic-Androgenic Steroids: Molecular and Organ Dysfunctions—A Critical Narrative Review for Forensic Interpretation
International Journal of Molecular Sciences · IF 4.9 · 2026-10-30
📄 Anabolic-androgenic steroids (AAS) comprise testosterone and synthetic androgenic compounds that differ substantially in pharmacokinetics, receptor activity, route of administration, hepatic metabolism, and clinical risk. From a forensic perspective, the central problem is not simply to catalogue adverse effects but to determine how verified exposure, chronology, co-use of other substances, clinical and electrocardiographic findings, pathological alterations, and toxicological results should be integrated when evaluating an individual death. This critical narrative review therefore distinguishes therapeutic testosterone replacement from non-medical and supraphysiological AAS exposure and examines the evidence connecting exposure to endocrine, cardiovascular, hepatic, renal, musculoskeletal, neuropsychiatric, and immunological findings. Human prospective and observational data support associations between androgen abuse and endocrine suppression, erythrocytosis, adverse lipid changes, increased blood pressure, and left-ventricular structural or functional alterations, with recovery of selected measured parameters reported in the studied cohorts. Electrocardiographic studies also report prolonged repolarization indices and impaired heart-rate recovery in AAS-using bodybuilders; however, these intermediate markers do not by themselves establish an individual risk of fatal ventricular arrhythmia. Forensic autopsy series describe myocardial hypertrophy, fibrosis, coronary disease, and other lesions in AAS-associated deaths, but these findings are not specific and require correlation with toxicology, clinical history, circumstances, and alternative causes. The detailed immunological literature is predominantly mechanistic, in vitro, or experimental and currently provides limited direct forensic evidence in human AAS users. Similarly, Raman spectroscopy is best regarded as a developing adjunct for forensic trace or analytical applications rather than a validated method for confirming AAS accumulation or cause of death in post-mortem tissue. We propose a structured forensic framework in which exposure is first verified, the temporal and pharmacological context is reconstructed, findings are assessed for specificity and plausibility, co-exposures and competing causes are examined, and the final opinion explicitly communicates the degree of certainty and the limitations of the evidence.

1Occupational Radiation Exposure During Robotic-Assisted Navigational Bronchoscopy: A Prospective Real-Time Dosimetry Study.
Journal of bronchology & interventional pulmonology · IF 3.2 · 2027-01-01 · PubMed
📄 Robotic-assisted bronchoscopy (RAB) uses fluoroscopy and cone-beam CT (CBCT), which can expose procedural staff to ionizing radiation. Real-time, role-specific occupational dosimetry data from live RAB procedures remain limited. We prospectively collected continuous personal dosimetry from attending physicians, anesthesia providers, and scrub nurses during 51 consecutive RAB procedures at a single academic center using a standardized low-dose imaging protocol and shielding. Collar-badge readings were converted to estimated effective dose using a conservative upper-bound conversion factor. Role-specific dose distributions were compared, and scenario-based annual estimates were generated by bootstrap resampling. Median per-procedure estimated dose was 1.27 µSv for attending physicians, 0.58 µSv for anesthesia providers, and 0.55 µSv for scrub nurses. Exposure differed by role, with the primary operator receiving the highest dose. CBCT cases were associated with higher staff and patient radiation measures but also involved smaller lesions and longer fluoroscopy times, indicating confounding by procedural complexity. Patient BMI was independently associated with physician exposure. Projected annual exposures remained well below the ICRP 20 mSv occupational limit across all roles. Occupational radiation exposure during RAB was low across all staff roles under an optimized imaging workflow. Role-specific dosimetry captures exposure differences across the procedural team and supports standardized low-dose imaging and shielding as core safety practices.

📄 Historically, the transfer of goods has been through sale, a model regulated by public legislation. Increasingly, however, the transfer of goods is occurring through licensing, a model regulated by private legislation. Privately-legislated licenses—for such chattels as musical and written works and agricultural goods—are being used to circumvent publicly-legislated restrictions on intellectual property. 📄 The neocortex has the largest volume, whereas the cerebellum houses the most neurons of the mammalian brain, underscoring paramount functions for interactions between them. Neocortical projections to the cerebellum involve both the mossy fibre and climbing fibre systems; mossy fibres find their origin predominantly in the pons, whereas the climbing fibres are relayed via subnuclei of the inferior olive (IO) that receive cortical input via the mesodiencephalic junction (MDJ). We reveal that mesodiencephalic junction neurons receive prominent excitatory inputs from neocortex and cerebellar nuclei, whereas their main inhibitory inputs are derived from the ventral tegmental area and periaqueductal grey. These findings shed light on how the activity of neurons in the mesodiencephalic junction can be regulated and how these neurons might influence activity in the inferior olive, and thereby cerebellar learning.
