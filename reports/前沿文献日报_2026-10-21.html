1A Spatial Interference Approach to Account for Mobility in Air Pollution Studies with Multivariate Continuous Treatments
Journal of the American Statistical Association · IF 3 · 2026-10-20
📄 We develop new methodology to improve our understanding of the causal effects of multivariate air pollution exposures on public health accounting for mobility. Typically, in environmental health studies, exposure to air pollution for an individual is assigned based on their residential address, though many people spend time in different regions with potentially different levels of air pollution. To account for this, we incorporate estimates of the mobility of individuals from cell phone mobility data to obtain a more accurate estimate of their air pollution exposure. We treat this as an interference problem, where individuals in one geographic region can be affected by exposures in other regions due to mobility into those areas. We propose policy-relevant estimands and derive expressions showing the extent of bias one would obtain by ignoring individuals’ mobility. We additionally highlight the benefits of the proposed interference framework relative to a measurement error framework to account for mobility. Utilizing flexible Bayesian methodology we develop novel estimation strategies to estimate causal effects that account for this spatial spillover. Lastly, we use the proposed methodology to study the health effects of ambient air pollution on mortality among Medicare enrollees in the United States.

F1000Research · IF — · 2026-10-20
📄 Background and objective Reducing low-value care may improve clinical practice and preserve scarce resources, but lower use alone does not demonstrate better allocation. We examined how selected African studies define inappropriate care, assess whether patients receive needed care, and report resource consequences and subsequent use. Methods We narratively reviewed English- and non-English-language clinical, implementation, economic, and health-system evidence. Database searches covered publications from 2000 through October 1, 2026, using PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, and African Journals Online (AJOL). We also searched citations backward and forward. We selected sources purposively to illustrate different clinical criteria, study designs, and resource outcomes, and linked reports from the same program. The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) methodologies over the past 5 years has created unprecedented opportunities for understanding and therapeutically targeting BMP4 biology at multiple scales, from atomic-resolution structure prediction to population-level clinical prognostication. Objective This review systematically examines the intersection of BMP4 biology and AI across three core domains: (1) AI-driven drug discovery targeting BMP4 signaling pathways, (2) deep learning-based protein structure prediction and computational modeling of BMP4 and its receptor complexes, and (3) clinical AI applications for BMP4-associated diseases. Methods A narrative review with systematic search elements was conducted across PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, bioRxiv, and arXiv for English-language publications from 2020 to 2026, supplemented by targeted searches via Semantic Scholar. Included studies were categorized as BMP4-specific, BMP receptor/BMP-pathway-targeted, or transferable AI methodology not yet applied to BMP4. Results We find that AI has accelerated BMP4-related research along multiple axes, though with important caveats. AlphaFold3 and related tools have generated high-confidence predicted models of BMP4-receptor complex architecture, aspects of which were previously intractable to experimental structure determination; these predictions remain to be fully validated experimentally. ML-guided virtual screening has identified first-in-class BMP pathway (receptor) modulators for FOP, although BMP4-specific small-molecule modulators have not yet been identified computationally. Conclusion The convergence of BMP4 biology and AI represents a fertile research Frontier. While foundational tools are now in place, realizing the full translational potential of AI-augmented BMP4 research will require coordinated efforts in data generation, model development, and rigorous experimental and clinical validation. We outline priority directions for the next 5 years (approximately 2026–2031).

📄 Deep State-Space Models (Deep SSMs) have shown promising performance and computational benefits in processing of time-series data with complex temporal dependencies. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) constitute a biologically inspired class of deep SSMs, where computing blocks communicate through spikes, discrete single-bit events rather than continuous activation functions. A particularly important task in time-series processing is forecasting, i.e. predicting future values of a sequence from past observations alone. However, how to best perform forecasting with SNNs is an open question, where the standard autoregressive nature of the data processing with SNNs requires values that are not available during the forecasting phase. In this article, we address this challenge. We explore two closed-loop forecasting strategies, in which the network's own generated values are fed back into the network as if they were observed past values: (i) model-level and (ii) layer-level closed-loop forecasting. We evaluate these approaches on the ETT-small datasets, a standard benchmark for time-series forecasting, under both spike-based and continuous activation functions. We compare the proposed closed-loop approaches against state-of-the-art methods such as SpaceTime, as well as the zero-masking baseline in S4. Both of the proposed approaches perform comparably to or better than zero-masking with S4, and in most cases match SpaceTime. These findings highlight the trade-off between continuous and low-bit quantized activations, and demonstrate that the proposed Deep SSM architectures provide a competitive framework for forecasting.

8Geometry-Agnostic Data-Driven Discovery of Extreme Scenarios
arXiv · IF — · 2026-10-20
📄 We introduce a two-stage data-driven neural framework for discovering dynamically admissible extreme scenarios. Rather than assuming prior knowledge of the governing equations, the first stage infers the effective dynamical constraints directly from observational data. When the underlying physical system possesses suitable symmetries, the learned dynamics can be represented in a geometry-agnostic form, independent of any fixed grid, coordinate system, dimensionality, and intrinsic curvature. In the second stage, a physics-informed neural-search uses this learned dynamics to uncover rare, high-impact coherent structures that may not have appeared in the original observations. We demonstrate this framework through representative examples of increasing dimensionality and geometric complexity. Starting from a traditionally learned Duffing dynamics, we find a homoclinic pulse as a plausible localized extreme scenario. Next, since the same equation also appears in the stationary nonlinear Schrodinger system, with the coordinate interpreted as space instead of time, this pulse becomes a standing soliton. We then retrain the first stage using a geometry-agnostic neural representation, enabling the learned dynamics to be lifted to higher-dimensional space to obtain higher-dimensional solitary profile, known as Townes soliton. Finally, with a Liouville system, we demonstrate that dynamics learned from data on a (1+1)-dimensional Lorentzian quasi-manifold can be transferred to a (2+0)-dimensional Euclidean manifold to identify an instanton. Our approach can be applied to other coherent spatiotemporal structures, including kinks, rogue waves, compactons, peakons, and potentially even singularities, with applications in risk analysis, nonlinear forecasting, and anomaly detection.
