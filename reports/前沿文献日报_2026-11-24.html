2AlphaFold models of host-pathogen interactions elucidate the prevalence and structural modes of molecular mimicry
PLoS Computational Biology · IF 3.6 · 2026-11-23
📄 Pathogens exploit host cellular machinery through protein-protein interactions (PPIs), often using molecular mimicry to hijack host cellular processes. While there have been thousands of host-pathogen PPIs determined to date, the lack of structural information for these impedes the study of the prevalence of molecular mimicry and convergent evolution of protein interaction interfaces. To address this, we benchmarked AlphaFold2 and 3 for prediction of structures of host-pathogen interactions observing that accurate models can be retrieved when ranking by modelling confidence, despite an overall low performance. This work showcases the value of large-scale structural modelling to study convergent evolution of host-pathogen interactions and how molecular mimicry may contribute to infection or host defense.

3When should adaptation arise from a polygenic response versus few large-effect changes?
PLoS Biology · IF 7.2 · 2026-11-23
📄 When will adaptation involve a few genetic changes of large effect versus a polygenic response? There are compelling reasons to expect polygenic adaptation to be common, yet direct evidence is lacking. Conversely, there are hundreds of examples of large-effect adaptations across species, but it is unclear whether they occur frequently within any given species. These different lines of evidence are hard to synthesize, in part due to the lack of theory that relates them. To address this gap, we frame the question in terms of the properties of an adaptive trait. We ask how the genetic basis of adaptation depends on key aspects of genetic variation in the trait and on the degree to which its selection pressures have changed. To this end, we consider a quantitative trait subject to stabilizing selection and model its response when a population at mutation–selection–drift balance experiences a sudden shift in the trait optimum. Using this model, we delimit how the contributions of large-effect and polygenic changes to adaptation depend on the genetics and ecology of adaptive traits, as well as on other salient factors. Thus, we formulate testable predictions for when different modes of adaptation are expected.

4A generalized alternating NGMRES method for PDE-constrained optimization problems governed by transport equations
Journal of Numerical Mathematics · IF 4 · 2026-11-23
📄 We propose a generalized alternating nonlinear generalized minimal residual method (GA-NGMRES) to accelerate first-order optimization schemes for PDE-constrained optimization problems governed by transport equations. Interpreting the update rule of a gradient descent scheme in the Hilbert space induced by the regularization model, i.e., a Sobolev gradient descent, as a fixed-point (FP) iteration, GA-NGMRES periodically alternates NGMRES updates with FP steps. It requires only first-order derivatives. We consider advection, mass-preserving transport, and incompressible flows as PDE models; variational regularization of the control variable promotes diffeomorphic transport maps, which we verify numerically. With a line search safeguard, GA-NGMRES converges globally. Since its FP map need not be contractive, we extend a recent analysis of NGMRES for noncontractive iterations to the alternating scheme. We compare GA-NGMRES to this Sobolev gradient descent, Anderson acceleration, a limited-memory BFGS method in the same Hilbert space, and Gauss–Newton–Krylov methods on real-world and synthetic data. GA-NGMRES converges where the unaccelerated scheme does not and outperforms Anderson acceleration. On our problems, it does not, in general, outperform the (quasi-)Newton methods, but it closes much of the gap to them; which method is fastest depends on the preconditioner, the regularization parameter, the data, and the stopping tolerance. GA-NGMRES is a simple and robust option when moderately accurate solutions suffice.

📄 Mental disorders and cardiometabolic diseases (CMDs) share genetic factors, but whether these genetic links are disorder specific or reflect genetic overlap among mental disorders remains unclear. 📄 To evaluate contraceptive effectiveness, discontinuation rates and reasons, and bleeding patterns among women who continued using the levonorgestrel 52-mg intrauterine device (LNG 52-mg IUD) for up to 10 years. We conducted a retrospective cohort study at the Universidade Estadual de Campinas School of Medical Sciences, Campinas, Brazil. I introduce DREAM, a differentiable calibration strategy in which the parameter-dependent operator is sampled offline by any legacy code, compressed by singular value decomposition, and reconstructed online in a differentiable framework so that automatic differentiation delivers exact likelihood gradients through the full forward solve at the cost of one additional evaluation per Hamiltonian Monte Carlo step.
