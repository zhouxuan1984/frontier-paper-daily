1AKT1-phosphorylated TERT assembles a FOXO3-MYC transcriptional complex that drives ERphagy and proteostasis in post-mitotic RPE
Autophagy · IF 14.3 · 2026-10-26
📄 The retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) sustains lifelong proteostasis under chronic stress, yet how post-mitotic cells activate autophagy when canonical kinase pathways suppress it remains unresolved. Here we demonstrate that AKT1, conventionally regarded as an autophagy suppressor, drives autophagy through a non-canonical transcriptional mechanism triggered by isoform imbalance. In RPE and age-related macular degeneration (AMD) models, AKT2 hyperactivation destabilizes mTORC2 and engages S6K-mediated IRS-1 inhibition, creating a feedforward autophagic arrest. Compensatory AKT1 activation via mTORC2 phosphorylates telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) at Serine 824, driving nuclear translocation that is independent of telomere maintenance. In the nucleus, TERT assembles with FOXO3 and MYC into a tripartite transcriptional complex that occupies the EIF2AK3 promoter, enabling PERK transcriptional activation. This activity converts the unfolded protein response from pro-apoptotic to cytoprotective: PERK-ATF4 signaling drives biogenesis of core autophagy machinery while simultaneously inducing selective ERphagy through the receptors TEX264 and CCPG1, which prevents pathological PERK clustering and preserves tubular ER architecture in diseased RPE. Using C. elegans phosphorylation-deficient mutants, mouse models and AMD patient induced pluripotent stem cell-derived retinal pigment epithelium (iPSC-RPE), we establish that AKT1-mediated TERT phosphorylation is an evolutionarily conserved prerequisite for FOXO/DAF-16 nuclear function and lysosomal homeostasis in post-mitotic cells. Pharmacologic targeting of AKT2 with a first-in-class dual-pocket allosteric inhibitor selectively enhances AKT1 compensation, restoring macroautophagic and ERphagy flux across disease models. These findings reveal a kinase-to-transcription axis that reprograms organelle quality control and identify the AKT1-TERT-PERK-ATF4 pathway as a therapeutic target in proteostasis-driven disease.

📄 Introduction Septic shock is a condition resulting from an uncontrolled immune response to infection, leading to multi-organ failure. Fluid resuscitation is the primary initial therapy, but an aggressive approach risks fluid overload and increased extravascular lung water (EVLW). To date, direct comparative evidence between fluid-based resuscitation strategies, vasopressors, or a combination of both on hemodynamic changes is limited. This study aims to compare hemodynamic responses to three resuscitation strategies in a randomized porcine model of endotoxin-induced septic shock. Methods This experimental protocol will include 15 male Sus scrofa domestica aged 16–20 weeks and weighing 40–60 kg, together with three non-septic controls as described in the source protocol. 4Evaluation of possibly fabricated references in 500,000 science, engineering, and computing papers
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · IF — · 2026-10-26
📄 References to nonexistent studies are well documented in biomedical research, but evidence outside biomedicine is limited. We analyzed a stratified random sample of 500,000 peer-reviewed journal papers in chemistry, physics, materials science, engineering, and computer science published from January 2023 through March 31, 2026, using OpenAlex, a large open index of scholarly works and citations. Our automated pipeline classified 1,135 references across 619 papers as fabricated, with 85.8% precision on masked expert review; fabricated is an operational label for a claimed title that could not be located in any of seven bibliographic and open-web sources. Affected-paper prevalence rose from 1 in 1,600 (6.3 per 10,000) in 2023 to 1 in 435 (23.0 per 10,000) in the first quarter of 2026, and full-period rates differed roughly sixfold across fields, from 25.6 per 10,000 papers in physics to 4.3 in chemistry. Most affected papers contained one or two fabricated references, but the 27 papers containing five or more accounted for more than a third of all fabricated references identified. Applying each field’s rate to the approximately 7 million OpenAlex papers indexed in these fields gives an estimated 8,500 papers carrying at least one fabricated reference. Routine automated reference verification at submission, together with retrospective screening of the published literature, can detect fabricated references before and after they enter the scientific record.

1Occupational Radiation Exposure During Robotic-Assisted Navigational Bronchoscopy: A Prospective Real-Time Dosimetry Study.
Journal of bronchology & interventional pulmonology · IF 3.2 · 2027-01-01 · PubMed
📄 Robotic-assisted bronchoscopy (RAB) uses fluoroscopy and cone-beam CT (CBCT), which can expose procedural staff to ionizing radiation. Real-time, role-specific occupational dosimetry data from live RAB procedures remain limited. We prospectively collected continuous personal dosimetry from attending physicians, anesthesia providers, and scrub nurses during 51 consecutive RAB procedures at a single academic center using a standardized low-dose imaging protocol and shielding. Collar-badge readings were converted to estimated effective dose using a conservative upper-bound conversion factor. Role-specific dose distributions were compared, and scenario-based annual estimates were generated by bootstrap resampling. Median per-procedure estimated dose was 1.27 µSv for attending physicians, 0.58 µSv for anesthesia providers, and 0.55 µSv for scrub nurses. Exposure differed by role, with the primary operator receiving the highest dose. CBCT cases were associated with higher staff and patient radiation measures but also involved smaller lesions and longer fluoroscopy times, indicating confounding by procedural complexity. Patient BMI was independently associated with physician exposure. Projected annual exposures remained well below the ICRP 20 mSv occupational limit across all roles. Occupational radiation exposure during RAB was low across all staff roles under an optimized imaging workflow. Role-specific dosimetry captures exposure differences across the procedural team and supports standardized low-dose imaging and shielding as core safety practices.
