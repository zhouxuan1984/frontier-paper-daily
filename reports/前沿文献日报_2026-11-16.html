1The brain tumor sequence registration (BraTS-Reg) challenge for establishing correspondence between pre-operative and follow-up MRI scans of diffuse glioma patients
Scientific Reports · IF 3.9 · 2026-11-15
📄 Abstract Registration of longitudinal brain MRI scans containing pathologies remains a challenging problem, due to substantial changes in tissue appearance. While medical image registration methods have advanced considerably, they often lack the required precision and robustness. To address this gap, we introduced the brain tumor sequence registration (BraTS-Reg) challenge, the first public benchmark specifically designed for deformable registration between pre-operative and follow-up scans of patients with adult-type diffuse gliomas. Conducted in conjunction with ISBI-2022 and MICCAI-2022, BraTS-Reg provides de-identified, multi-institutional, multi-parametric MRI data accompanied by expert-annotated landmark points across time. Top-ranked methods demonstrated comparable performance and shared key features, including pre-alignment of scans, deep neural networks, inverse consistency analysis, and per-case test-time optimization. The best method achieved MEE comparable to inter-rater variability for 63% of landmarks, underscoring the need for further improvements in accuracy and robustness relative to human experts. BraTS-Reg serves as a three-fold active resource for research on image registration, providing a publicly available dataset, a framework for a community benchmark, and online evaluation tools, accessible at https://bratsreg.github.io/ .

📄 Abstract Layered double hydroxides (LDHs) are increasingly explored for agricultural, environmental, and biodelivery applications, but their composition-dependent effects on mammalian cells remain insufficiently defined. The formulations showed hydrodynamic diameters of approximately 200–300 nm, moderate dispersity, strongly positive surface charge, and characteristic lamellar LDH features. Together, these findings show that LDH biological activity cannot be generalized across metal compositions and that high-content phenotypic profiling provides added sensitivity for detecting early cellular perturbations. This integrated approach supports composition-aware nanosafety evaluation and may inform the safer development of LDH-based technologies for agricultural and biotechnological applications.
