3Alcohol use reduces the efficacy of anti-PD1 immunotherapy by disrupting antitumor immunity
Science Signaling · IF 6.6 · 2026-11-17
📄 Immune checkpoint inhibition (ICI) has improved outcomes for some patients with advanced malignancies, but relatively few have a complete response. Alcohol use is common and impairs adaptive immunity. Here, we explored the link between alcohol use and ICI efficacy in mouse models of lung and bladder cancers and performed a retrospective assessment of a cohort of patients with cancer receiving ICI therapy. In both mouse models, a regimen of moderate-to-heavy ethanol intake reduced ICI efficacy by ∼50% and increased tumor recurrence. In patients with lung cancer, regular alcohol use was associated with reduced survival benefit from ICI. Effects were less definitive in patients with bladder cancer, melanoma, and head and neck cancer, in whom alcohol use reporting was too limited. In mice bearing MB49 bladder tumors, ethanol intake reduced the number of tumor-infiltrated T cells favoring T helper 17 cell (T H 17 cell) over T H 1 cell phenotypes in tumor-associated CD4 + T cells. It also reduced the number of tissue-resident memory T cells, increased the proportion of T cells expressing the alternative immune checkpoint receptor TIGIT, and induced SREBP-mediated lipid signaling in tumor-associated T cells. Blocking TIGIT with antibodies or, to a lesser extent, inhibiting SREBP signaling with betulin improved the efficacy of PD1 receptor blockade. The findings suggest that regular alcohol use negatively affects ICI efficacy, which may be counteracted by cotargeting TIGIT. Future incorporation of patients’ alcohol use in cancer databases will help define its effect on the outcome of various therapies in multiple cancers.

International Journal of Surgery · IF 10.1 · 2026-11-17
📄 Background: Body composition measurements may predict surgical outcomes, but current approaches are not optimized across body regions and outcome types. The added value of these measurements beyond established clinical factors remains unclear, highlighting a critical gap. Methods: This retrospective cohort study conducted across three hospitals within a tertiary health system evaluated whether computed tomography (CT)-derived body composition metrics predicted postoperative outcomes after colectomy, alone or in combination with clinical variables or the American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (NSQIP) Surgical Risk Calculator. The largest benefit was in NSQIP-classified low-risk patients (predicted mortality <5%), where the C-index increased by 4% ( P < 0.001), suggesting that image-based frailty scores can identify at-risk individuals who would otherwise be overlooked. Conclusions: CT-derived body composition metrics strongly predict colectomy outcomes, particularly in patients considered low risk preoperatively. Integrating these metrics with existing risk calculators can improve surgical risk stratification and guide preoperative decision-making.

📄 Abstract Neural activity and eye movements are well-established predictors of human memory performance. 📄 Abstract Background An important application of single-cell genomics experiments is to identify genes specific to biological categories or experimental conditions. We develop ember (Entropy Metrics for Biological ExploRation), an entropy-based framework that uses Shannon’s chain rule across nested biological partitions. 📄 Determining crystal symmetry from powder x-ray diffraction is a central problem in materials characterization, yet multiple space groups produce indistinguishable patterns, and automated methods plateau at low accuracy on real data.
