1Language modeling on tabular data: a survey of foundations, techniques and evolution
Artificial Intelligence Review · IF 13.9 · 2026-10-17
📄 Tabular data, a prevalent data type across various domains, presents unique challenges due to its heterogeneous nature and complex structural relationships. Achieving high predictive performance and robustness in tabular data analysis holds significant promise for numerous applications. Influenced by recent advancements in natural language processing, particularly Transformer architectures, new methods for tabular data modeling have emerged. Early techniques concentrated on pre-training Transformers from scratch, often encountering scalability issues. Subsequently, methods leveraging pre-trained language models like BERT have been developed, which require less data and yield enhanced performance. The recent advent of large language models, such as GPT and LLaMA, has further revolutionized the field, facilitating more advanced and diverse applications with minimal fine-tuning. Despite the growing interest, a comprehensive survey of language modeling techniques for tabular data remains absent. This paper fills this gap by providing a systematic review of the development of language modeling for tabular data, encompassing: (1) a categorization of different tabular data structures and data types; (2) a review of key datasets used in model training and tasks used for evaluation; (3) a summary of modeling techniques including widely-adopted data processing methods, popular architectures, and training objectives; (4) the evolution from adapting traditional Pre-training/Pre-trained language models to the utilization of large language models; (5) an identification of persistent challenges and potential future research directions in language modeling for tabular data analysis. 5Impact of matched samples on domain harmonization in cross-cohort NMR metabolomics
Metabolomics · IF 3.3 · 2026-10-17
📄 INTRODUCTION: Machine learning models trained on metabolomics data frequently show reduced performance when applied to independent cohorts because of domain shifts introduced by differences in analytical platforms, sample handling, storage conditions, and metabolite quantification procedures. Robust approaches for harmonizing metabolomics datasets across cohorts are therefore needed to improve model transferability and reproducibility. OBJECTIVES: To evaluate the impact of matched samples on domain harmonization and cross-cohort prediction performance in NMR metabolomics data, and to compare multiple harmonization strategies for improving model generalization across independently acquired datasets. METHODS: A machine-learning framework was developed to classify ICD-10 code I50.9 (unspecified heart failure) using plasma NMR metabolomics data from the Estonian Biobank. Models were trained on a source dataset comprising 12,000 samples measured at Nightingale Health and evaluated on an independently measured destination dataset of 295 samples acquired on a different NMR platform. Harmonization approaches included within-domain normalization, Optimal Transport, Bridge mapping, and Empirical Bayes correction. The effect of matched samples was assessed using up to 108 duplicated biospecimens measured in both datasets. Additional validation was performed using independently generated human gestational diabetes and mouse diabetes metabolomics datasets from Metabolomics Workbench. RESULTS: Domain harmonization generally improved cross-cohort prediction compared with unaligned data, particularly when matched samples were available. Predictive performance increased as the number of matched samples used for calibration increased, although gains varied across methods. Nevertheless, simple within-domain normalization consistently provided strong performance and often matched or exceeded more complex alignment approaches. External validation using fully unmatched human and mouse metabolomics datasets demonstrated that harmonization strategies improved classification performance relative to raw data, supporting the broader applicability of the framework. CONCLUSION: Matched samples substantially enhance domain harmonization and improve the transferability of metabolomics prediction models across analytical platforms and cohorts. However, robust normalization remains a strong baseline, and the benefits of more sophisticated alignment methods depend on the availability and representativeness of matched reference samples. These findings provide practical guidance for developing reproducible cross-cohort metabolomics machine-learning models while preserving biologically relevant signals.

📄 Drug discovery has evolved from a largely empirical discipline into a knowledge-intensive enterprise that depends on the integration of pharmacology, structural biology, computational chemistry, and bioinformatics. Modern pharmacotherapeutic design is no longer satisfied by the demonstration that a molecule binds a macromolecular target. Instead, a candidate must be considered within a broader context that includes disease mechanism, network position of the target, tissue expression, structural tractability, off-target liabilities, pharmacokinetic behavior, and the probability of translation from preclinical systems to humans. Particular emphasis is placed on the limitations of current approaches, including database incompleteness, bias in training sets, uncertain biological annotation, and the common error of overinterpreting docking scores or predictive probabilities as proof of therapeutic success. The central argument is that the most reliable drug design strategies emerge when computational breadth is combined with pharmacological depth. In that integrative framework, bioinformatics helps define what should be tested, while pharmacology determines what the resulting signals actually mean for drug action, safety, and translation.

1Channelrhodopsin ion selectivity determines mechanisms and efficacy of optogenetic defibrillation in human atria and ventricles
The Journal of Physiology · IF — · 2026-10-17
📄 Abstract Cardiac re‐entrant arrhythmias are a major cause of sudden cardiac death. Optogenetic defibrillation has emerged as a promising anti‐arrhythmic strategy based on photoactivation of channelrhodopsins, yet the efficacy and potential adverse consequences of activating different classes of light‐gated ion channels have not been systematically evaluated. Previous studies assessed either cation (e.g. ChR2) or anion (e.g. GtACR1) non‐selective channels, both of which depolarize resting cardiomyocytes. Recently identified light‐gated K + channels (e.g. WiChR) suppress cardiomyocyte activity while maintaining the membrane potential near its resting state, but their defibrillation potential remains unexplored. We used computational simulations to compare ChR2, GtACR1 and WiChR. Single‐cell simulations showed that ChR2 and GtACR1 markedly increased diastolic intracellular Ca 2+ (by 42.6% and 52.6%, respectively), whereas WiChR induced only minimal changes (by 4.0%), suggesting lower pro‐arrhythmogenic risk. WiChR, however, slightly increased intracellular Na + (by 15.1% vs . 0.1% and 3.4% for ChR2 and GtACR1), consistent with the residual Na + permeability of currently available K + ‐conducting channelrhodopsins. Simulations in human ventricles and atria showed that GtACR1 most effectively terminated re‐entrant arrhythmias at low light intensities, whereas WiChR achieved comparable efficacy at ≥5 mW/mm 2 . Complementary tissue‐scale simulations revealed that defibrillation occurs through depolarization within the excitable gap followed by fast Na + channel inactivation (ChR2 and partly GtACR1), or through reduced membrane resistance at sufficiently high light intensities (large‐conductance channels GtACR1 and WiChR). Overall, we demonstrate that channelrhodopsin ion selectivity is a key determinant of both optogenetic defibrillation efficacy and its underlying mechanisms. image Translational perspective Re‐entrant arrhythmias are a leading cause of sudden cardiac death. Standard therapies, including the activation of implantable cardioverter‐defibrillators, carry burdens such as pain and myocardial injury. Optogenetic defibrillation has been suggested as a potentially gentler alternative, replacing high‐voltage electrical shocks by photoactivation of light‐gated ion channels expressed in cardiac cells. Determining which light‐gated ion channel optimally balances efficacy and adverse effects on cardiac electrophysiology is critical for clinical translation. We addressed this question using computational simulations and validation experiments in zebrafish hearts. We identify ion selectivity as a key determinant of both the success rate for arrhythmia termination and the underlying mechanisms: non‐selective cation‐ and anion‐conducting channels terminate re‐entry efficiently while promoting intracellular Ca 2+ accumulation, a plausible substrate for pro‐arrhythmic side effects that could undermine therapeutic safety. K + ‐conducting channels largely avoid this pro‐arrhythmic risk by holding cardiomyocytes near their resting potential, albeit at the cost of requiring transmural illumination, while also causing mild Na + accumulation attributable to residual Na + permeability. Together, these trade‐offs define clear clinical implications: K + ‐conducting channels may provide the safest option for optogenetic defibrillation, particularly once variants with further enhanced K + selectivity and red‐shifted activation spectra for improved operation within blood‐perfused myocardium become available. Key points We use computational simulations to compare cation non‐selective (ChR2), anion non‐selective (GtACR1) and K + ‐selective (WiChR) channelrhodopsins for optogenetic termination of re‐entrant arrhythmia. Single‐cardiomyocyte simulations suggest that ChR2 and GtACR1 activation can cause progressive accumulation of intracellular Ca 2+ , which is minimized when using WiChR. Intracellular microelectrode recordings from hearts of transgenic adult zebrafish validate the simulated voltage dynamics seen with ChR2, GtACR1 or WiChR activation. Simulations of human left ventricles and atria indicate that GtACR1 is most effective in terminating re‐entrant arrhythmia at low light intensities, whereas WiChR becomes similarly effective at higher intensities. Tissue‐scale simulations indicate distinct defibrillation mechanisms: excitable gap extinction by de novo action potential initiation followed by inactivation of fast Na + channels for depolarizing channelrhodopsins (ChR2, GtACR1), and reduction in membrane resistance for the large‐conductance channels (GtACR1, WiChR), effectively clamping the membrane potential at each channel's reversal potential at high light levels.

8Localizing and quantifying cerebral arterial stenosis from sparse velocity waveforms using a conditional neural operator
arXiv · IF — · 2026-10-16
📄 Cerebral arterial stenosis alters blood flow throughout the vascular network, complicating the estimation of its location and severity from sparse velocity observations. We developed a conditional neural operator that predicts cerebral hemodynamics for candidate stenoses and enables inverse estimation through waveform comparison. Training data were generated using a sequential 0D-1D model that simulates pressure, cross-sectional area, and velocity under varying anatomical, physiological, and stenosis conditions. The operator encodes a single stenosis within vessel tokens and uses global attention to represent its effects across the network. Severity-gated coupling connects these representations to a shared field decoder, while paired healthy simulations constrain baseline predictions. After training, model weights remain fixed, and stenosis location and severity are estimated by minimizing the mismatch between predicted and observed midpoint velocity waveforms. Network-averaged relative validation errors were 0.14% for pressure, 0.59% for area, and 2.79% for velocity. For an eight-vessel observation configuration, localization accuracy across 20 candidate vessels was 99.4% without added noise and 96.0% at the 30% noise setting. Comparisons between configurations with equal numbers of observed vessels showed that measurement placement influenced recovery. Errors and localization ambiguities were greater in selected communicating and distal arteries. These results demonstrate the feasibility of surrogate-based stenosis estimation in simulated cerebral networks and highlight the importance of observation placement. Evaluation was limited to unseen stenosis scenarios within sampled anatomical and physiological conditions. Generalization to new individuals and clinical measurements requires further investigation.
