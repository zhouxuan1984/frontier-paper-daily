   - 📄 摘要：Background Multivariable Mendelian randomization estimates the causal effect of multiple exposures on an outcome, typically using summary statistics of genetic variant associations. However, exposures of interest in Mendelian randomization applications will often be measured with error. Classical measurement error will increase the standard errors of the genetic association estimates. With a single exposure, this will result in bias toward the null in a two-sample framework. However, this will not necessarily be the case with multiple correlated exposures. Methods We show how bias arises when multivariable Mendelian randomization is conducted using genetic associations with exposures measured with error. We examine the direction and size of this bias, as well as coverage, power and type I error rates across various simulation settings. We use a maximum likelihood approach to account for the measurement error. Results Measurement error on exposures in a multivariable Mendelian randomization analysis may cause effect estimates to be biased in any direction and can also lead to inflated type I error rates and under-coverage. Furthermore, when multivariable Mendelian randomization is used in a mediation analysis, measurement error may lead to an underestimate of the proportion of an effect which is mediated by other factors. In an applied example looking at the effects of body mass index and waist-to-hip ratio on coronary heart disease, our proposed method attenuates the causal effect estimate for body mass index while increasing the estimate for waist-to-hip ratio. This is consistent with a scenario where waist-to-hip ratio is measured with error and there is bias in effect estimation using standard methods. Conclusions Measurement error on exposures in multivariable Mendelian randomization studies can lead to considerable bias in effect estimation and incorrect conclusions in hypothesis testing. By incorporating the covariance matrix of the genetic variant-exposure association estimates, the bias due to measurement error can be accounted for using a maximum likelihood approach. This approach also retains nominal type I error rates and coverage. This provides an important sensitivity analysis for multivariable Mendelian randomization studies.
